How the weekly rule works — and its limits

  • Scan every Friday close (or the last trading day of the week on a short holiday week) across the whole universe, rank qualifying breakouts by volume surge, take the top {{ weeklyTopN }}.
  • Pre-order fill — each pick is filled at the following Monday's open, not Friday's signal price (that's what actually happens with a pre-order).
  • Exit — stop or target touch during the week, else force-closed on the week's last trading day. That forced exit is approximated by that day's close — daily candles don't have a real 2:30 PM price, so this is a stand-in, not an exact fill.
  • Capital — your weekly capital is split evenly across all {{ weeklyTopN }} slots every week, whether or not all {{ weeklyTopN }} find a qualifying pick (an empty slot just sits idle that week — capital is not compounded week over week).
  • Charges — STT, stamp duty, exchange fees, GST and the flat ₹16 DP charge are deducted per round trip, so "Net profit" is what would actually reach your account. Slippage is still not modeled, so real fills remain slightly worse.
  • Survivorship bias — the scan universe is today's Nifty 500, so delisted and demoted companies are invisible. This tilts historical results optimistically.
  • Past results are not a guarantee of future ones.

How the intraday books work — and their limits

  • Opening range breakout — let the first hour (09:15–10:15) trade out and mark its high and low. After that, the first candle to close beyond the range takes the trade: above the high goes long, below the low goes short.
  • Support / resistance — the opposite bet. Previous-day pivots give S1/R1; a bar that dips to S1 but closes back above it buys the bounce, a bar that pokes R1 but closes below it sells the rejection. Target is the central pivot.
  • Slots — positions fill strictly in the order signals fire, first come first served. Ranking the day's signals by which turned out best would be look-ahead bias: at 10:20 you cannot know which stock breaks out at 14:00.
  • Capital — split evenly across slots, but each position is kept above ₹2,500, so smaller capital concentrates into fewer names rather than positions too small to outrun their own charges. No MIS leverage is modeled (1× only).
  • Exits — stop, target, or forced square-off at 15:15. If a bar touches both stop and target, the stop is taken (conservative). Intraday charges are deducted per trade.
  • Slippage is not modeled and the universe is today's Nifty 50, so results still lean optimistic. Past results are not a guarantee of future ones.

How the search protects you from itself

  • Pick which elements to include and what values to try. Every combination becomes a complete strategy, and all of them get tested.
  • The window is split. The earlier 60% is where combinations are searched; the later 40% is held back and never used for choosing. A strategy only counts as promising if it also works on data it was never fitted to.
  • Why that split matters: test 300 combinations on one dataset and a few will look excellent by pure chance. That is not edge, it is the lottery having a winner. The held-back period is what separates the two.
  • Cost hurdle — each result shows the avgR it must clear just to pay its own charges. Anything below that line loses money however good the win rate looks.
  • Entries fill at the next bar's open, never the signal close. Stop beats target when one bar spans both.
